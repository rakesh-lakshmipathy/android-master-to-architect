# Project walkthroughs and leadership stories

## Project walkthrough: CRAFT

- **Context:** product, users, scale, team and your role.
- **Requirements:** constraints, quality attributes and problem evidence.
- **Architecture/action:** your decisions and implementation/coordination.
- **Failure/trade-off:** alternatives, risk, incident or what did not work.
- **Tangible result:** measured outcome and learning.

Prepare 2-minute and 8-minute versions. Be precise about “I” versus “we.” Draw current architecture and one critical flow. Know traffic/data/device scale, bottlenecks, security, testing, rollout, incident and what you would change.

## Leadership story bank

Prepare evidence for:

- influencing without authority;
- resolving technical disagreement;
- high-severity incident and follow-up;
- migration/refactor with measurable result;
- mentoring/delegating and growing ownership;
- saying no or reducing scope;
- technical-debt investment;
- cross-team/API conflict;
- failure/mistake and changed behavior;
- ambiguous decision under time pressure.

Use STAR-L: situation, task, action, result, learning. Add constraints, options and why. Results can include risk prevented, lead time, reliability, build time, accessibility, support or team autonomy—not only revenue.

## Resume bullets

```text
Weak: Worked on modularization and Compose.
Strong: Led checkout extraction across 4 teams; introduced versioned contracts
and staged migration, reducing affected-module build time 35% while holding
payment error guardrails below 0.1%.
```

Never invent precision. State how measurement was obtained and confounders. A failed project can be a strong story if ownership, containment, learning and changed system are concrete.

## Follow-ups to rehearse

What exactly did you decide? Who disagreed? What data changed minds? What failed? What was your personal contribution? How did you measure? What trade-off remains? What would you do differently? How did the team operate after you left?
