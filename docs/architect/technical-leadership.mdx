# Mentoring, design reviews, and alignment

Technical leadership increases the system's and team's ability to make good decisions without the architect becoming a bottleneck.

## Design review packet

Require problem/user, goals/non-goals, constraints/assumptions, current state, quality attributes, options, proposed data/control flow, contracts, security/privacy, failure/recovery, observability, migration, testing, rollout, open questions and decision owner.

Review early enough to change direction. Invite affected mobile/backend/product/security/QA/operations/accessibility owners, but distinguish contributors from decision maker. Time-box unresolved debate, run a focused spike when uncertainty is empirical, and record the decision/conditions in an ADR.

## Mentoring ladder

1. Explain the principle and context.
2. Demonstrate one example.
3. Solve the next example together.
4. Let the engineer lead while you observe/review.
5. Let them teach/document it.

Tailor support: direct instruction during incidents; questions and delegation during growth work. Give specific behavior-impact-next-step feedback privately; recognize impact publicly. Do not use production-critical work as an unbounded learning exercise without safety nets.

## Cross-team alignment

Create stable decision forums, ownership maps, API/version policies, RFC/ADR templates, deprecation windows, shared scorecards and escalation paths. Alignment is not unanimous agreement. It is shared context, an explicit decision, committed execution, and a way to revisit when assumptions change.

## Practical mistakes

- ❌ Architect designs alone and announces the solution.
- ✅ include implementers/operators and surface constraints early.
- ❌ become required reviewer for every change.
- ✅ establish principles, owners and delegated decision boundaries.
- ❌ mentor by rewriting someone's code silently.
- ✅ explain risks and let them own the improved solution.

## Exercise

Facilitate a 45-minute design review for offline checkout. Define pre-read, attendees, questions, decision rule, parking lot, and recorded outputs.
