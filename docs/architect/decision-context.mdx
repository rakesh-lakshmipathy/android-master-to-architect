# Requirements, constraints, and quality attributes

Architecture begins by discovering what must be true—not by selecting MVVM, Clean Architecture, or a database.

## Separate inputs

- **Functional requirement:** user can place an order offline.
- **Quality attribute:** 95% of cached catalog opens under 300 ms on supported low-tier devices.
- **Constraint:** payment authorization requires network/server verification.
- **Assumption:** users may remain offline for seven days.
- **Invariant:** one idempotency key never creates two orders.
- **Non-goal:** real-time inventory accuracy while offline.

Turn “fast,” “secure,” “scalable,” and “reliable” into scenarios containing source, stimulus, environment, artifact, response and measurable response. Example: “When connectivity returns after seven days with 500 queued mutations, sync completes without duplicate orders and keeps UI responsive; p95 completion is under two minutes on the reference device/network.”

## Stakeholder and evidence discovery

Interview product, users/support, backend, security/privacy, QA/accessibility, operations and release owners. Inspect incidents, telemetry, app/version/device distribution, current contracts, budgets, policies and team skills. Mark uncertainty and confidence; do not convert guesses into facts by putting them in a diagram.

Prioritize quality attributes because they conflict: stronger freshness can cost battery/network; richer abstraction can cost build/cognitive time; stricter security can add UX/recovery complexity.

## Practical mistakes

- ❌ “Must use microservices/MVI” as a requirement.
- ✅ state the problem/quality attribute that might justify it.
- ❌ list every quality as highest priority.
- ✅ force ranking and document sacrifice thresholds.
- ❌ hide assumptions in implementation.
- ✅ record owner, evidence, confidence and validation date.

## Exercise

Rewrite “Build a scalable secure chat app” into functional requirements, measurable quality scenarios, constraints, assumptions, invariants and non-goals.
