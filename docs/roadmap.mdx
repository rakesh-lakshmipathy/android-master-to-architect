# Android Master to Architect — complete roadmap

This is the curriculum for the entire book. It is arranged in learning order: language foundations first, Android internals next, then UI, architecture, data, device capabilities, production engineering, and architect-level decision-making.

**Status:** ✅ Written and clickable &nbsp;&nbsp; ◐ Rewrite in progress &nbsp;&nbsp; ○ Planned

<Admonition type="info" title="How to use this roadmap">
Do not try to memorize everything in one pass. Learn a topic, run its examples, answer its interview questions, complete its practical exercise, and revisit it while building a real feature. A topic is marked complete only after it meets that standard.
</Admonition>

## Definition of a completed lesson

A topic becomes ✅ only when it contains:

- A plain-English explanation of **what it is and why it exists**
- A small example followed by a realistic production scenario
- Step-by-step execution and expected behaviour
- Practical ✅ Prefer and ❌ Avoid comparisons
- Lifecycle, process-death, concurrency, security, and performance implications where relevant
- Corner cases and failure handling
- Runnable Playground links for every independent Kotlin/JVM example
- Clear Android-only labels for framework examples
- Interview-ready answers, follow-up questions, and exercises
- Useful diagrams, decision tables, or checklists

<Admonition type="tip" title="The destination">
Mastery is not remembering every API. An Android architect can explain how the platform behaves, choose an appropriate design under real constraints, predict failure modes, measure results, communicate trade-offs, and guide a team toward a maintainable system.
</Admonition>
